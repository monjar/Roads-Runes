# The world: The Old Roads

> **Superseded in part (2026-10-04).** How the app talks (buttons, labels,
> errors, the Watch, the ride's voice) is now `docs/VOICE.md`: simple, warm
> fantasy, not the dry understatement below. Its glossary replaces the Lexicon

The fiction every player-facing line in Roads & Runes belongs to, and the
rules for writing more of it. The plan it serves is `docs/ROADMAP.md`; the
longer drafts it was cut from are in `docs/design/rpg/drafts/` (superseded
where they disagree with this page).

## The premise

**One line** (store, welcome): Every road was written once. Go out and read it back.

**One paragraph** (the prologue):

> Every road was written once, by people who cut a rune where two ways met.
> People still use the roads. Nobody reads them. A road that is used and not
> read goes vague: that is the fog. Things settle in the vague parts. You read.

**The first codex entry, "The Old Roads":**

> Every road was written once. The people who made them cut a rune where two
> ways met, and the rune said what the road was for: this one to water, this
> one to market, this one home. The land read the runes and kept to them. That
> was the arrangement.
>
> The people were waywrights, and there were four trades of them. They are
> covered elsewhere and would want it noted that they got on.
>
> People still use the roads. Nobody reads them. Signposts do the work, and a
> signpost says where a road goes without saying what it is for. A road that is
> used and not read goes vague. That is the fog. It is not weather and it is
> not dangerous. It is ground that has stopped being sure of itself.
>
> Things settle in the vague parts. They are small and local and have habits.
> None of them is in charge.
>
> The cure is not complicated. Go out and read. What you have read goes in the
> book, and the book does not forget; that is what it is for.
>
> E. Sallow, who was asked to keep this short.

## The trades

Class ids and names stay (Explorer, Wizard, Warrior, Scribe). The noun in copy
is **trade**, never class or order. Each trade has a guild, named only in its
description and its codex page.

| Class | Guild | Believes | Saying | Crest | Posts its notices |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explorer | the Wayfinders | A road nobody has taken is a rumour. | "The edge moves." | A compass star whose north-east point runs long, two hatch strokes below | Nell Foss |
| Wizard | the Cutters | Every straight line was somebody's idea. | "Look twice, then once more." | A rough standing stone, one stave cut down its face, three short rays | Enid Sallow |
| Warrior | the Menders | A road is kept with the legs. | "The hill does not negotiate." | A mattock upright over a single hill line, a notch cut in the slope | Tam Hurdle |
| Scribe | the Clerks | What is not written down was never there. | "It may as well be you." | An open book, a road drawn across both pages and off the edge | Walter Garth |

Founders, for codex pages only: Agnes Fell (Wayfinders, "walked off the county
map and sent back a corrected one"), Tobias Marrow (Cutters, "could read a
milestone by hand in the dark and was wrong about most other things"), Hester
Cragg (Menders, "carried the same stone up the same hill until it stayed"),
Edmund Pell (Clerks, "wrote down the weather for forty years and is the only
reason anybody knows it rained").

They write; they are never described and never answer. One name per card.
Each writes in a grammar of their own, so a line can be told without its
byline.

## The runes

Twenty-four, in four sixes. The Codex shows them in futhark order (three eights)
with the game's sixes on top. Each rune has one fixed gloss and, where it can be
cut, a **road form**: what it comes out as when the knife is a road. "A stave
cannot be ridden."

| Six | How met | Runes |
|---|---|---|
| **Road** | found lying anywhere | Raido (loop) · Sowilo (zigzag) · Kenaz (triangle) · Dagaz (square) · Ansuz (a note) · Wunjo (a stop) |
| **Ground** | found only on their own kind of ground | Laguz · Berkano · Eihwaz · Jera · Ehwaz · Algiz |
| **Trade** | given by the cast at chapter ends | Fehu · Gebo · Mannaz · Tiwaz · Perthro · Othala |
| **Hard** | taken from the old ones and elders | Uruz · Isa · Nauthiz · Hagalaz · Thurisaz · Ingwaz |

The glosses are in `backend/app/lore/config/runes.json`. A star is "a bind",
several runes cut as one: always optional, never required.

**How runes are drawn.** Plain Elder Futhark forms only: never doubled, never
beside themselves, never on a share card, widget, icon, crest or title ribbon.
Sowilo, Othala, Tiwaz and Algiz never appear alone at display size outside the
Codex. Othala means what is kept, not home or heritage.

## Real places

The narrator may be British. The ground may only be what the map's tags say.

- Creature lore describes the creature's belief, never the place's history.
  "Guards a fire that went out long ago. Nobody has told it." Not "Guards a
  place that burned down."
- A landscape word in a line about a real place comes from that place's tags or
  is generic: "the bank", "the water", "the line". Never "the lock" unless the
  map says lock.
- No age, history or condition claims next to a real place's name: "before the
  road", "older than", "burned", "drowned", "a ruin" (unless tagged so).
- Nothing settles, and no joke is made, at a memorial, grave, place of worship
  or hospital.
- Nothing is ever asked for at a road junction. Copy says pass through, never
  stop.
- "Home ground" is a label shown to the player alone. It is never stored in
  text, put in a title, sent to a model or shared.

## Voice

**Rules**

1. Plain declaratives, short words, under twenty words a sentence.
2. Second person, present tense.
3. Things want, charge, keep, remember and wait. People mostly do not.
4. British spelling and furniture where the map allows it: towpath, verge,
   kerb, bins.
5. Understate. The biggest thing in the game is "a long way".
6. Numbers under thirteen in words in prose; digits in tallies and prices.
7. Real things keep their real names. Everything else is "the pond", "the
   bridge".
8. The joke is in the facts, never in the wording.
9. The scholar admits doubt: "the map says", "nobody is sure".
10. Mid-ride: one sentence of five words or fewer, name first ("Fen Troll.
    Wants a climb."). A second sentence only for a number.

**The turn is allowed, not required.** The two-beat line, a flat statement
and then a turn, is the house sound ("Mostly wall. The moss is the dangerous
part."). It wears thin if every line does it:

- One turn per screen. If a cast line is on the card, the body is plain.
- The more often a surface is seen, the plainer: the reckoning, the journal
  entry and the loading line turn at most one time in three.
- Never a joke on a rule, a number, a price, an error, a permission, a safety
  line, a broken run of days or a sensitive place.
- Jokes belong where a thing is met once: codex entries, item lines, first
  meetings, titles, chapter endings.

**Never**

- exclamation marks;
- archaic diction: thou, hark, behold, realm, destiny, hero, chosen,
  traveller;
- invented place names, or an epithet fused to a real name;
- invented facts about real places;
- speed as praise: fast, sprint, race, personal best;
- gore: slay, kill, blood, death;
- other players, ranks, comparison;
- emoji and decorative symbols;
- "ancient", "mystic", "arcane", "epic" or "legendary" as praise;
- surface jargon: AC, HP, loot, buff, spawn, tier, mob;
- cheerleading: "Great job", "Amazing";
- more than three sentences on a card.

**Right and wrong**

| | Right | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| The turn | "Mostly wall. The moss is the dangerous part." | "A fearsome golem of living stone, cloaked in deadly moss." |
| Understating a level | "Level nine. The roads know you a little better." | "LEVEL UP! You are becoming a legend." |
| Effort, not speed | "It wants the road used. Take your time." | "Outrun it: hold 24 km/h for a kilometre." |
| Lore without invention | "A ruin, by the map's account. Something has been keeping the nettles down." | "Built in 1140 by a forgotten baron, the abbey fell to fire." |
| Mid-ride | "Fen Troll. Wants a climb." | "A Fen Troll stirs beneath the old bridge ahead, traveller." |

**Locale.** Seasons flip by hemisphere and their names are configurable. A
player outside Britain still sees the British narrator, but never a British
noun the map does not support.
